Introducing myself

Who I am, how I got here, and what I care about in this field.

I'm Matt — a Technical Architect originally from Methuen, Massachusetts, currently based in Volta Redonda, Brazil. I wanted this first post to be simple: a bit of background on how I got into software, and what's kept me here.

How I got started

My path started in IT support, not software engineering. In 2018 I was fixing school computers and thin-client setups as an intern, and by 2019 I was a Technical Support Analyst untangling ERP issues — database problems, tax and regulatory logic, and training end users to actually use the systems we built for them. That hands-on exposure to how businesses actually run on software is something I still draw on today.

From there I moved into junior engineering roles at Transporte Excelsior and UniFOA, building and modernizing internal systems — fleet management, inventory, reporting — while finishing my degree in Information Technology. It was slow, practical work: migrating legacy applications, introducing version control where there wasn't any, and learning what "maintainable" really means once you're the one maintaining it.

Going deeper into engineering

At Icatu Seguros I got my first real taste of distributed systems and enterprise APIs at scale, in an industry — insurance — that doesn't forgive sloppy architecture. Then at Ambev Tech, over two and a half years, I worked on real-time logistics systems: anomaly detection, status tracking, and high-performance APIs running on Azure with Docker and Kubernetes inside a SAFe agile organization. That's where I really learned to own systems end to end — from architecture through CI/CD to what happens when something breaks at 2am.

Kinder Morgan was a shift in stakes. I led engineering on the DART platform used across their gas pipeline operations, touched SCADA logic and scheduling engines, and built a custom regression testing tool for stored procedures that made database deployments something the team could trust instead of dread. Working on critical infrastructure changes how you think about correctness.

Where I am now

Today I'm the Technical Architect and Lead Engineer for Railwhiz at Automate Army, building AI-powered analytics and reporting on top of large-scale GPS traffic processing pipelines. I've also been building internal developer tooling, including a custom AI coding agent platform based on Pi-Mono, and doing cross-functional architectural consulting across the org.

Underneath all of it, what actually pulls me into this work is the stuff most people never see: compilers, parsers, and computer architecture. Some of my side projects — a small expression parser for C# REST APIs, a lexical analyzer — are just me chasing that interest for its own sake. I think that curiosity about how systems work at the lowest level is what makes me better at the business-critical stuff on top.

What to expect here

I'll be writing about architecture decisions, things I learn building and maintaining production systems, and the occasional deep dive into compilers or language design when I can't help myself. Thanks for reading — more soon.
