Introducing myself

Where I started, what I do now, and what this blog is going to be.

I'm Matt. Technical Architect, originally from Salem, New Hampshire, currently living in Volta Redonda, Brazil. Here's the short version.

I started in support

Not engineering. In 2018 I was an intern fixing school computers. By 2019 I was an ERP support analyst — database bugs, tax rules, and users who just needed the thing to work. It taught me how businesses actually run on software, which isn't something you learn from a codebase.

Then I built

Junior roles at Transporte Excelsior and UniFOA: fleet management, inventory, reporting. Migrating legacy apps. Adding version control where there wasn't any. Finishing my degree in the middle of it. Unglamorous, and probably the most useful stretch of my career.

Icatu Seguros introduced me to distributed systems. Ambev Tech introduced me to scale — real-time logistics, anomaly detection, Azure, Kubernetes, and the occasional 2am page. At Kinder Morgan I worked on DART, a platform used across their gas pipelines. Correctness reads differently when pipelines depend on it.

Where I am now

At Automate Army I wear a lot of hats. My work spans R&D — internal AI tooling, coding, platform — along with Railwhiz and customer projects, and the team working across them. Most weeks are some mix of architecture, code, and figuring out what's actually worth building.

Why I'm writing

Under all the job titles, the thing that actually pulls me in is compilers, parsers, and computer architecture. My side projects are mostly me chasing that. So this blog will be architecture notes, whatever production teaches me, and the occasional compiler tangent. More soon.
